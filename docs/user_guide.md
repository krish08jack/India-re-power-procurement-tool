# User Guide

## 1. Purpose

The India Renewable Energy Power Procurement Tool helps users evaluate renewable energy procurement options using cost, project economics and energy matching analysis.

---

# 2. Getting Started

Open the Streamlit application.

Enter the required project and procurement assumptions.

The tool calculates the relevant results based on the inputs provided.

---

# 3. Version 0.1

Version 0.1 focuses on renewable energy cost comparison.

### Typical workflow

1. Enter renewable plant capacity.
2. Enter CUF.
3. Enter CAPEX.
4. Enter O&M cost.
5. Enter project life.
6. Enter discount rate.
7. Enter applicable grid and Open Access costs.
8. Review LCOE.
9. Compare renewable electricity with grid electricity.
10. Review estimated savings.

---

# 4. Version 0.2

Version 0.2 provides long-term PPA economics.

Users can evaluate:

- PPA tariff
- Tariff escalation
- Project generation
- Generation degradation
- O&M costs
- Revenue
- Cash flow
- NPV
- IRR
- Payback

The model evaluates the project over the specified project life.

---

# 5. Version 0.3

Version 0.3 evaluates monthly renewable energy matching.

Users provide or select monthly demand and renewable generation assumptions.

The model evaluates:

- Direct renewable energy use
- Monthly surplus
- Monthly shortfall
- Banked energy
- Bank withdrawal
- Remaining grid requirement
- Renewable energy share

---

# 6. Understanding the Results

### LCOE

Average levelised cost of renewable electricity over the project life.

### NPV

Present value of project cash flows after accounting for the discount rate.

### IRR

Return generated by the project based on its cash flows.

### Payback Period

Approximate period required to recover the initial project investment.

### Renewable Energy Share

Percentage of electricity demand served by renewable energy.

### Surplus

Renewable electricity available beyond the electricity demand during a period.

### Shortfall

Electricity demand that cannot be met by renewable generation during a period.

### Banked Energy

Eligible surplus renewable energy carried forward for later use, subject to the applicable assumptions.

---

# 7. Interpreting Results Carefully

A lower LCOE does not necessarily mean that a procurement option is always preferable.

Users should consider:

- Cost
- Long-term project economics
- Renewable energy utilisation
- Demand matching
- Banking
- Grid dependency
- Regulatory conditions
- Contract structure

The tool is intended to support decision-making rather than replace detailed project analysis.

---

# 8. Recommended Workflow

For a complete procurement assessment:

Step 1 → Evaluate renewable electricity cost

Step 2 → Compare with grid and Open Access costs

Step 3 → Evaluate PPA project economics

Step 4 → Assess monthly renewable energy matching

Step 5 → Examine surplus and shortfall

Step 6 → Evaluate banking requirements

Step 7 → Review financial and energy indicators together

Step 8 → Conduct sensitivity/scenario analysis before making a final decision
